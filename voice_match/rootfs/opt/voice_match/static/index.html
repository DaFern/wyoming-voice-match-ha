Personal instructions

For example: “Call me Daniel. Keep answers brief and use Celsius.” These are response preferences, not permissions or executable templates. Do not put secrets here. When enabled, they are sent to your selected Codex provider with your request.

Learning a voice

Record only through the authorised learning connector, using its microphone at your normal speaking distance. Read each phrase naturally for about five seconds. Ordinary Assist microphones do not supply training samples.

Wait for the sample to save before recording again. If rejected, try the same phrase again. Exact words are not checked; existing audio format and duration checks still apply. Only the consenting speaker should use the learning microphone.

Pausing keeps partial samples for you to resume with fresh consent or delete later. Normal voice matching stays separate throughout learning.

Add your voice

Isolated learning is a development prototype. Starting or resuming requires a trusted speaker connector, which is not implemented yet. Normal Assist traffic cannot train a voice. Do not switch an ordinary assistant to a learning pipeline as a workaround.

We use about 30 short samples. The saved voiceprint stays on this system. A voice match is not proof of identity or permission to perform sensitive actions.

Privacy

Your signed-in Home Assistant account controls which voices you can manage. Administrators can manage all profiles. Other users can only manage their own.

Deleting samples keeps your voiceprint. Deleting a voice removes both. Neither action removes old backups or guarantees secure erasure from disk.

New microphones or a changed voice may need fresh samples. Keep the original Whisper assistant available as a fallback.

Codex personalisation pairing

Administrator setup. The existing STT endpoint stays unchanged. Pair Codex Multi-Assist first, then connect a separate Wyoming integration to this add-on on port 10352 and use it only with the paired agent. An unpaired agent cannot interpret the encrypted request context.

These stricter checks affect preferences only, not existing speech filtering. Scores are not probabilities. Accepted but uncertain voices receive neutral preferences and fresh context. Voice matching is not authentication.
